Overview

Secure Nest is a smart home security app that lets users monitor, control, and protect their homes from a single platform. It combines real-time surveillance, device automation, energy insights, and an AI assistant, with easy multi-user access. I designed the key user flows, UI screens, and a verification test plan to ensure a secure and seamless experience.

Context & Backstory

Smart home automation redefines how we interact with our living spaces by connecting devices like lights, locks, and cameras into a unified ecosystem. This technology allows homeowners to monitor and control their environment seamlessly through mobile apps or automated routines, making daily life more convenient and personalized.

Beyond convenience, it enhances safety and energy efficiency. Users can access real-time alerts, live security feeds, and remote appliance management to optimize their home's performance with intelligent insights.

“Multiple apps and devices—one complicated routine” “Fragmented solutions put homes at risk.”

Problem
Definition

As connected homes continue to grow in popularity, the need for intuitive, user-centered interfaces becomes essential to ensure that technology feels seamless, accessible, and genuinely helpful in everyday life.

Disconnected Devices

Many homeowners use multiple device apps and platforms. This fragmentation creates friction and inconsistent control across the home.

Fragmented Platforms & UX

Overlapping, inconsistent interfaces lead to confusion and poor discoverability of core features and automations.

Security & Monitoring Gaps

Traditional security measures and scattered device alerts create delayed responses and leave homes vulnerable.

Complex Daily Management

Routine tasks (lighting, climate, locks) require repetitive manual steps when automation could simplify them.

Key Findings

1. Fragmented Device Management

Users struggle with switching between multiple apps for lights, cameras, locks, and appliances. This leads to confusion and wasted time.

2. Security Awareness Is Unreliable

Many users miss important alerts because notifications get mixed with regular phone updates. They want clearer, real-time security alerts.

3. Difficulty Setting Up Automations

Most users avoid automation features because they find the setup complicated. They prefer simple, guided routines or templates.

4. Desire for a Unified Experience

Across all age groups, users expressed a strong need for a single, easy-to-use app that brings everything together seamlessly.

Analyzing the Indian Smart Home Landscape

MARKET
RESEARCH

India's smart home market is expanding rapidly, yet remains highly fragmented. Most current solutions focus on individual devices, leaving homeowners without a unified platform to manage their ecosystem intuitively.

The Integrated Gap

Currently, no platform unifies control, security, and energy insights for Indian households. Users frequently face:

  • ● Fragmented Apps Across Multiple Brands
  • ● Lack of Real-Time Security Alerts
  • ● Limited Visibility into Energy Usage
Understanding the competition and market standards. Discover Phase • Competitive Audit

COMPETITIVE
AUDIT

I analyzed three major players in the Indian smart home market to identify gaps and opportunities for a truly unified and user-centric solution.

Bharat Smart Home App

Primary focus: Indian households, with a strong focus on energy monitoring + basic automation (lights, fans, ACs).

Key features: Track electricity usage, identify wastage, schedule devices, set timers and routines, advanced "Bijili Auditor" analytics.

Strengths
  • India-specific energy context (bills, savings).
  • Good for users wanting basic control.
Limitations
  • Relatively narrow ecosystem compared to others.
  • Users still juggle brand-specific apps.

Samsung SmartThings

Primary focus: Large, multi-brand ecosystem hub for smart TVs, appliances, sensors, lights, cameras, etc.

Key features: Single app to connect, monitor and control; routines & automation, geofencing, AI Energy Mode, 3D map view.

Strengths
  • Strong interoperability with many brands.
  • Deep integration with Samsung ecosystem.
Limitations
  • Works best with Samsung/SmartThings devices.
  • Mixed low-cost brands still need separate apps.

Xiaomi Home (Mi Home)

Primary focus: Acts as a hub for the Xiaomi / Mi smart ecosystem – TVs, air purifiers, cameras, lights, plugs, sensors, etc.

Key features: Add/manage devices, real-time status, remote control, smart scenes/automation, device-sharing with family.

Strengths
  • Works well if users are in Xiaomi's ecosystem.
  • Good automation via scenes + budget devices.
Limitations
  • Optimization is mainly for Xiaomi products.
  • Mixed brands still create fragmentation.
